# Operating Standard: Partner and Internal Communication Timelines

This is a worked artifact, not the workflow description itself — it's what "Execution" and "Administration and Monitoring" look like as an actual document a new hire could follow on day one, not just a paragraph in the README. Every figure below is illustrative, adapted from the general shape of an operating standard rather than lifted from a real one.


## Processing timelines by complexity tier

Grants are not all the same weight, and promising one turnaround time for all of them either overpromises on the complex ones or underpromises on the simple ones.


**Standard grants** (routine, low-risk, prior relationship on file): a target processing window measured in a small number of weeks, tracked against a rolling median rather than a worst-case estimate.


**Grants requiring enhanced due diligence** (higher-risk relationship, first-time grantee, or a structure needing additional documentation): a noticeably longer, multi-week window that gets revisited whenever the diligence process itself changes.


**Restricted-structure grants** (a legal or regulatory structure that adds required steps beyond the standard agreement): a timeline held for internal planning purposes rather than promised externally, since it depends on how much of the standard process the structure can skip versus how much extra due diligence a risk policy adds back in.


The standard here isn't any specific week count — it's the practice of tracking real turnaround data by tier and updating the promised timeline against that data on a regular cadence, instead of letting a stale number sit in an FAQ document indefinitely.


## When a policy change makes the timeline unpredictable

The hardest communication problem isn't a slow process — it's a process that just changed and hasn't produced enough data yet to know its new speed. The standard here: when a new requirement (added documentation, a new risk review, a new signature step) makes a previously-reliable timeline unreliable, stop promising the old number rather than defend it past its expiration date. Say plainly that a firm timeline can't be given yet, commit to monitoring the data, and set a date — a specific number of months out — to report back with a real number.


## Grantee communication cadence

**Standard communication:**


- Initial outreach to the grantee within 1 business day of assignment.
- Follow-up on outstanding material on a fixed cadence, roughly weekly.
- If a grantee goes silent through several consecutive follow-ups, escalate to the referring relationship holder (the advisor, program officer, or account owner) and ask them to re-establish contact.
- If that escalation doesn't produce a response within a further short window, the grant is returned to the referring party rather than held indefinitely in a pending state.
- All communication attempts and outstanding items are logged in the grant record itself, not just in email, so the history is visible to anyone who picks up the file.
- Grantee emails get a same-day-or-next-day response.

**Expedited grants:**


Assignment and initial outreach both happen within a business day — the same commitment as standard grants, but with no queue delay before the clock starts.


## Coverage workflow


- Staff out of office for an extended stretch transfer their full pending caseload to a named backup before they leave.
- Staff out for a shorter stretch only transfer specific files that have an urgent, date-driven deadline landing during their absence.
- Regardless of how long someone is out, the team lead is the fallback point of contact for anything time-sensitive that surfaces while they're away.

## Internal communication standard


- Internal messages get a next-business-day response under normal volume.
- During a known seasonal peak (year-end deadlines, a major renewal cycle), that window is explicitly extended, and the extension is communicated to the team in advance, not discovered when someone's message goes unanswered.

## Why this belongs in the model

Field Readiness for Execution and Administration and Monitoring isn't "we have a diligence process" — it's a documented standard specific enough that a new hire could follow it without asking their manager what "usually" happens. A tiered turnaround commitment, a defined escalation ladder, a coverage plan, and a rule for what to say when a timeline becomes genuinely unpredictable are what turn a workflow description into something the team can actually be held to.
